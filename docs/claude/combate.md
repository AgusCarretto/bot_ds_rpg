**Co-op zone bosses — the ENGINE (`RaidModule`; until v0.18.x it was the `/raid` command)**: `Modules/RaidModule.cs` + `Services/RaidSessionService.cs`. Same
in-memory philosophy as solo combat, but a *different concurrency model on purpose*: solo combat's
`ICombatSessionService.TryAdvance` is a compare-and-swap that **rejects** the loser of a race — right
for one player double-clicking, wrong for a raid where several different players clicking "Atacar" at
once is the normal case. So a `RaidSession` is mutable and every mutation happens under
`RaidSession.Lock` (plain `lock`, so **no `await` inside it** — do the I/O after releasing).
Rules that must keep holding: (1) the phase flips to `Resolved`/`Activating` *inside the same lock*
that decides the outcome — doing it afterwards let a concurrent click trigger a second victory and
double rewards; (2) HP is persisted exactly once per participant (on flee, or at raid resolution),
never incrementally, or the delta gets applied twice; (3) fleeing forfeits the reward, like solo;
(4) `IRaidSessionService`'s player→raid index and `RaidSession.Participants` must never disagree: the
starter is added to `Participants` in `BuildSessionAsync` (they used to be registered in the index but
not the roster, so they couldn't join, couldn't start, and stayed locked out of `/hunt` after the
lobby expired), `Remove` releases players *by the index* (not by iterating `Participants`), and
`TryActivateAsync` closes the raid if it throws midway. (The old "Known gap: an `Active` raid has no
inactivity timeout" is closed since v0.19.0: the turn clock above retires whoever does not play.) Raid difficulty is not the stored boss stats: the raid boss is
`GameData/RaidDifficulty.cs` (HP +50% per extra player, plus per-zone multipliers) applied *at activation*
once the roster is final (`RaidSession.BossBaseHp` keeps the unscaled roll), so the lobby's provisional
`BossMaxHp` is meaningless until the phase flips to `Active`. The multipliers depend on the zone's **rank by
difficulty** (`RaidSession.ZoneRank`): zone 1 keeps ×2.2 HP / ×1.35 damage (calibrated on its softer boss), zone 2+
uses ×1.5 / ×1.1 because those bosses are already tuned to the zone ladder — stacking the zone-1 multipliers on a
ladder boss made the raid impossible (0-3% win). Whenever a zone boss is retuned, re-measure its raid (`dotnet run --project tools/balance2 -- K`, per zone, levels min+3/8/15, 1–4 players). **Measured 2026-10-19 (nothing moved since v0.17.1)**: at arrival level (min+3, own gear, no food) a 2-player raid wins only 18–28 % in Zones 2–5 and a 4-player one 44–62 %, while the solo boss with food and ability wins ~79/79/70/45 % — at arrival level the raid is the HARDEST way to face the boss, and only from min+5/6 on it becomes the easiest (min+8: 73–94 % with 2 players). **v0.18.6 lowered the raid HP ×0.75 (1.76 → 1.32 and 1.2 → 0.9) and v0.19.0 UNDID it before it ever reached production**: the owner (2026-10-19) does not want the team boss easier; he wants the win rate to rise with every extra player (+50 % HP per extra player, `HpPerExtraParticipant`) so that fighting it in a group is promoted. Measured with the pre-v0.18.6 life (`tools/balance2 -- K … 1.3333`, own gear, NO food because the team fight has none, zones 2–5, 400 per cell): at arrival level (min+3) 1 player 0–1 %, 2 players 21–29 %, 3 players 40–55 %, 4 players 45–59 %; at min+8 2 players 74–94 %, 3 players 90–100 %, 4 players 96–100 %. With ONE meal per player (`K … 1.0 heal` on today's code; it was `1.3333` while v0.18.6's ×0.75 was in the code; the meal is implemented since v0.19.0, and banquete variants `bNNN` / `buff` / `pre` exist in `PartK`): arrival 2 players 30–71 %, 3 players 51–89 %, 4 players 57–92 %; min+8 2 players 72–100 %, 3 players 90–100 %. For reference the solo boss with food and ability wins ~79/79/70/45 % at arrival level, so without food a duo is a worse bet than going alone, and the open lever to make the group clearly better is allowing a meal in the team fight (see `MEJORAS.md`) (the two constants in `RaidDifficulty.cs`; see `MEJORAS.md`). The numbers quoted just above for "zone 1 ×2.2 / zone 2+ ×1.5" are the ORIGINAL calibration, kept for history.
Nobody can be in a solo fight and a raid at once (`AdventureCombatStarter` and `RaidModule` check
both `ICombatSessionService` and `IRaidSessionService`). Shared helpers extracted for reuse:
`GameData/ZoneRanking.cs` (zone order by `min_level`, never raw `zone_id`) and
`GameData/PlayerCombatProfileCalculator.cs` (level + gear + class passives → combat stats).

**Class abilities & the single turn resolver**: each class has one active ability with a button in
manual combat (`GameData/ClassAbilities.cs` — every balance number lives in `AbilityTuning`, 3-turn
cooldown for all four). The turn itself (player strike → Sifón de Almas → monster counter, with
ability effects applied) is resolved in exactly **one** place, the pure
`GameData/CombatTurnResolver.cs`, used by solo combat (`AdventureModule.ResolveTurnAsync`), raids
(`RaidModule.ResolveParticipantTurn`), `/use` mid-fight (`ResolveCounterTurn`) and `/autohunt`. Do
**not** re-inline that math in a module — it used to be copy-pasted in four places and adding
abilities to four copies was the reason it got extracted. Two deliberate rules: `/autohunt` never
uses abilities (design decision — it's for AFK farming of weak mobs; strong play means manual
combat), and callers must check `CheckAbility` before requesting `PlayerAction.Ability` (an invalid
request throws). Per-fight ability state (`AbilityState`) lives in `CombatState.Ability` /
`RaidParticipant.Ability`. In a raid the button is a generic "Habilidad" (the message is shared, so
it can't be labelled per player).

**Typed combat commands (v0.14.5; `aa attack|atk|atacar`, `aa ability|skill|hab`, `aa flee|huir`; `Modules/TextCommandModule.CombatActions.cs`, `Services/CombatTurnOutput.cs`, `Services/RaidTurnOutput.cs`)** — the owner: the buttons sometimes "se trancan" (Discord gives the bot 3 s to answer a click, and when the bot is slow the click says "no respondió" and the turn is not played). A text command has no such deadline, so the same turn can be played by typing, in `/hunt`, `/travel`, `/boss` and `/raid`. **There is ONE turn implementation**: `AdventureModule.ResolveTurnCoreAsync` (solo) and `RaidModule.ResolveAttackCoreAsync` / `ResolveFleeCoreAsync` (raid) are static and have no `Context`; the button handler and the text command both call them, and only the *output* differs (`ICombatTurnOutput` / `IRaidTurnOutput`): the button EDITS the clicked message and answers ephemerally; the typed command sends a NEW message with NEW buttons and strips the buttons of the old one (`ICombatReplyTarget.ClearComponentsAsync`, a default interface method). For the solo fight the new message does not exist yet when `TryAdvance` asks for the next reply target, hence `SwitchableCombatReplyTarget`, re-pointed after the send; in a raid the shared message is `session.ReplyTarget`, which is re-pointed (the old buttons also keep working, their ids carry the raid id, not the message). `IRaidSessionService.RaidIdOf(discordId)` finds the player's raid. It only helps while the fight still exists in memory: **the per-turn timeout is 60 s since v0.14.5 (it was 30)** (`CombatSessionService.TurnTimeout`; without acting in that time the fight ends as "Combate abandonado") and a bot restart (every deploy to `main`) drops every fight. Harness: `typedtest`.

**Fleeing asks first (v0.37.1; `AdventureModule.HandleFleeAsync` / `HandleFleeYesAsync` / `HandleFleeNoAsync`, `RaidModule` equivalents, `SessionTargetCombatTurnOutput` / `SessionTargetRaidTurnOutput`, `CombatState.FightId`, harness `v0371test`; NO migration)** — the owner (2026-10-29): "cada vez que se huya de una batalla necesito que se pregunte, ¿estás seguro?, cobarde o algo así… porque el guerrero al editar el mensaje en el celu me tira el huir donde está el atacar: si vas atacando rápido tocás sin querer". On a phone the message is edited after every turn and the "Huir" button can land where "Atacar" was, so a fast tap fled by accident. Now the **Huir button (`btn_flee`, and `raid_flee:{id}` in the team fight) does NOT flee: it answers a PRIVATE (ephemeral) message "🐔 ¿Estás seguro? Huir es de cobardes…" with two buttons, «No, a pelear» (green, first) and «Sí, huir» (red, second)**. The fight message is NOT touched (so its buttons do not move: that is the whole point of using a separate message and not swapping the buttons in place). «Sí, huir» (`btn_flee_yes:{fightId}` / `raid_flee_yes:{raidId}`) runs the SAME flee core as before (`ResolveTurnCoreAsync(fled: true)` / `ResolveFleeCoreAsync`) but with an output that edits the fight's OWN message (the session's `ReplyTarget`), since the click comes from the private message, and then closes the private one ("🏃 Huiste de la pelea." / "No se pudo huir: mirá el aviso."); «No» just closes it ("💪 ¡Seguís en la pelea!"). **`CombatState.FightId`** (a new Guid per fight, set by `AdventureCombatStarter`, kept by every `with`) travels in the ids: if the fight the player wanted to leave already ended and another began, the old «Sí» answers "esa pelea ya terminó… no huiste de nada" instead of fleeing the new one (the raid id is already unique per raid). **Typed `aa flee` / `aa huir` does NOT ask**: typing it is already deliberate (it is also what the owner uses when the button hangs). Duels' «Rendirse» was not asked for and was not changed. The strings are translated (`Locales/en.json`); the team-fight ones are Spanish only like the rest of `RaidModule`.

**The fight lock: in the middle of a fight only fight commands work (v0.25.4; `GameData/FightLock.cs`, `Services/FightGuard.cs`, the two hooks in `Program.cs`, harness `v0254test`)** — the owner (2026-10-23): "bloqueá todos los comandos si estás en medio de pelea, exceptuando los de la pelea como el use… no quiero que pueda recolectar madera en plena pelea". A player is "in a fight" when `FightGuard.IsInFight` says so: a solo combat (`ICombatSessionService.Peek`), a team boss that is already fighting (`IRaidSessionService.IsInActiveFight`, not the invitation lobby) or a duel (`IDuelService.IsInDuel`). Then `FightLock.IsAllowed(commandName)` is an ALLOW-list: **use, attack, ability, flee** (the fight itself; the combat BUTTONS are components, never commands, so they are never blocked) and, because they only look, **profile, inventory, cd, info, tutorial**. Everything else answers `FightLock.Message` ("⚔️ Estás en plena pelea: no podés usar `/chop`…", private for slash) and is not dispatched. It is an allow-list on purpose: a NEW command is blocked during a fight until someone decides otherwise, instead of slipping through. ONE place for everything, before dispatch: `HandleInteractionAsync` (slash, by the top-level `CommandName`) and `HandleTextMessageAsync` (text: the command is found with `_textCommands.Search` and named by its PRIMARY alias, so `atk`/`u`/`p` follow `attack`/`use`/`profile`; an unknown command is not blocked and takes its usual "no reconozco ese comando" path). `v0254test` compares the allowed names against the real `[SlashCommand]` and `[Command]` attributes, so renaming a command without updating the list fails the harness. Buttons and select menus of other scenes (tavern, missions...) are NOT blocked: only commands were asked for. If a ghost fight ever locked someone, it clears itself (the solo timeout is 60 s, the team turn clock retires the inactive, a restart empties everything). **Also in v0.25.4 — why "no tenés ningún combate activo" shows up**: `CombatSessionService.RecentEnd` remembers how each player's last solo fight ended (`timeout` after 60 s without playing, or `resolved`), and `AdventureModule.NoActiveFightMessage` says so ("pasó más de 1 minuto…", "ese ya terminó… mensaje viejo") or, with no record, blames the bot restart (fights live in memory, every deploy cuts them) or another running copy of the bot (the same token on two machines makes both answer every command); it also prints a `[COMBATE]` line to the log with the cause, so Railway's console shows how often it happens and why.

It is ONE SQL statement (`WITH up AS (INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE ... RETURNING (xmax = 0)), kit AS (INSERT INTO inventory ... WHERE inserted)`): the kit goes only with a genuinely NEW `users` row, 12 simultaneous clicks hand it out exactly once, and a Fuego Nuevo or a class change never repeats it. `SetClassAsync` stays for tests/old callers; `/start` uses `CreateAccountAsync`. The owner **declined** two other ideas from the same audit — a "low HP" tip in the victory embed and honest "fragile at the start" class descriptions ("ya dice qué tiene cada clase") — so do not add them.

**PvP: friendly duel (`/fight @user`, `aa fight @user`) and the daily Arena (`/arena join|listplayers|results`)** — both run on ONE pure engine, `GameData/DuelEngine.cs`, built on the
same two halves of the combat resolver the monster fights use (`CombatTurnResolver.ResolveStrike` = "I hit" and `ResolveIncoming` = "I get hit"; `ResolveTurn` is just the two glued
with a monster counter, and its behavior did not change — `abilitytest` checks the distributions). PvP-only rules, all in that file: turns alternate; the defender's DEFENSE is subtracted
from the attack BEFORE abilities/crits (subtracting it after made Bola de Fuego hit ~4× a plain strike against a defended rival instead of its calibrated 2.2×); lifesteal heals on damage
that actually landed; an ability effect (Aguante/Sombra) is spent by hits received. **Class balance is PvP-specific** (`GameData/PvpTuning.cs`): per-class combat-HP factors (Guerrero 0.90,
Hechicero 1.20, Arquero 1.25, Ninja 1.0) — without them the Warrior beat every class 74–85% and the Archer lost 80–85%, so any tournament would be won by a Warrior. They were
measured with 3000–4000 simulated duels per matchup at levels 5/12/22/30 (policy "use the ability as soon as it is ready"); with one lever (HP) the floor is ~66/34 in the worst matchup and
a rock-paper-scissors forms (Guerrero > Ninja > Arquero/Hechicero > Guerrero). **Re-measure whenever a class passive or ability changes** (the harness is a double loop over `DuelEngine.Simulate`).
The friendly duel (`Modules/DuelModule.cs`, `Services/DuelService.cs`): challenge with Accept/Decline buttons (2 min, one open challenge per challenger), then alternating turns with
Attack / the class ability / Forfeit buttons, 60 s per turn (the one who does not play loses); both start at full HP and **nothing touches the database** (no HP, gold, XP or
cooldowns — only `duel_win`/`duel_loss` events). A `DuelSession` is mutable under its `Lock` (no `await` inside; same model as the raid), the winner/loser bookkeeping runs once when the
session is released, and the turn timer races the click inside that same lock. **The Arena** (`Services/ArenaService.cs`, `Repositories/ArenaRepository.cs`, tables `arena_days/arena_entries/arena_matches`, `Database/add_arena.sql`):
one tournament per **Uruguay day**; people join during the day (max 32, min 2; joining is a transaction that locks the day row so the cap can't be passed); the bot's clock
(`Modules/ArenaScheduler.cs`, every 30 s, started from `ReadyAsync`) plays every open day BEFORE today — so it runs at 00:00 Uruguay, or right when the bot comes back if it was off at midnight. The bracket is single
elimination (`GameData/ArenaBracket.cs`, pure: shuffled order, the first slots get byes when the count is not a power of two), fights are `DuelEngine.Simulate` with the stats the players have AT THAT MOMENT,
and closing the day is ONE transaction: `UPDATE arena_days SET status='resolved' ... WHERE status='open'` (the guard — 20 concurrent closers pay once), the matches, and the champion's prize via
`RewardPayer` (15 hunts of gold of THEIR zone + 6% XP + a zone box, `ArenaRules.ChampionReward`). Fewer than 2 players = cancelled, no prize (so nobody farms the daily prize by joining alone). The result is
NARRATED in the channel where the first player joined (or `Arena__ChannelId`), round by round: an opening message with the roster, one message per round (each fight gets a line from `GameData/ArenaNarrator.cs` — a crushing win, "by a hair" (the winner finished with <=15% HP), a very long fight — chosen deterministically from the fight, using the stored `arena_matches.winner_hp_pct`) and a closing message that pings the champion with the prize and the whole bracket; `ArenaModule.BuildBroadcast` builds the messages (pure) and the clock sends them with `ArenaScheduler.RoundPause` (4 s) between them. **The Arena has luck since v0.23.2 (`GameData/ArenaTuning.cs`, `GameData/ArenaStory.cs`, harness `v0232test`)** — the owner (2026-10-22): "la Arena la gana siempre el de más nivel… quiero que pueda ganarlo alguien de nivel 1 contra el del 20, si no se hace imposible… random con textos mientras pasan duelos". Measured with `DuelEngine.Simulate` (same class and gear): without luck +1 level won 65–90 % and +5 levels 89–100 %, and a level 1 with NO gear against a level 20 with Zone 2 gear NEVER won, because the defense is subtracted flat from every hit and leaves the underdog at the minimum damage — so a few points of luck are not enough, and the fix is three layers, only in the Arena (`/fight` keeps real power): (1) **power compression** `PowerCompression = 0.5` — in each pair the damage, defense and HP move toward their geometric mean (`mean × (v/mean)^0.5`); (2) **form of the day** `FormSwing = ±30 %` — each fighter rolls a damage-and-HP factor for THAT fight; (3) **miracle** `MiracleChance = 30 %` — in 3 fights of 10 the pair is fought at EQUAL power (compression k = 0). Measured (4 classes, 1,600+ fights per cell, higher level wins): even 50 %, +1 ≈ 55 %, +5 ≈ 64 %, +10 ≈ 71 %, +19 ≈ 78 %, and level 1 without gear vs level 20 with Zone 2 or Zone 4 gear: the level-1 wins ≈ 14–15 %.
